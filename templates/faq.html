Is my email leaked? How to check and what to do

Updated: November 2026 · CyberLord Domain Intelligence

Most account takeovers do not start with clever hacking. They start with a leaked email address and a password that was reused somewhere else. If your email appears in a breach, criminals already have a working key to try against your bank, your social accounts and your email itself, usually within hours. The good news is that a leak is survivable if you move fast. This page answers the questions people ask most about email leaks, in plain language, and points you to a free check you can run right now.

Is my email leaked?

The fastest way to find out is to run a free check. The CyberLord email exposure check looks your address up in infostealer-compromise intelligence, which is data collected from computers infected by password-stealing malware. If your email shows up, you will see when it was first seen and how many saved accounts were tied to it. That tells you exactly how urgent the cleanup is.

How do I know if my email has been compromised?

Run the free check first, but also watch for warning signs. Password reset emails you did not request. Login alerts from places you have never been. Friends saying they received strange messages from you or a business account. Email forwarding rules you did not create. A mailbox that sends mail while you are asleep. Any one of these means you should treat the address as compromised and act the same day, not next week.

What should I do if my email was leaked?

Work from a clean device, ideally one that has never had unknown software installed on it. Change your email password first, because email controls the reset of everything else. Then change every account that shares that password, starting with banks, payment apps and anything with money attached. Turn on two-factor authentication everywhere you can, especially on email and banking. Check your email settings for forwarding rules and filters you do not recognise, and sign out of all other sessions. If you run a business, tell customers whose data may have been affected, since Nigerian law under the NDPA expects honest communication about breaches.

Does a clean result mean my email is safe forever?

No, and anyone who promises that is not being straight with you. A clean result means your email did not appear in the infostealer-compromise data we checked. That source covers infostealer infections, not every historical breach on the internet. New compromises happen all the time. Recheck every few months, keep passwords unique so one leak cannot unlock everything, and use a password manager so unique passwords stop feeling hard.

Can someone use my leaked email to hack my bank?

Yes. This is exactly how it works in practice. Attackers take leaked email and password pairs and try them against banks, fintech apps and crypto wallets automatically, in bulk, around the clock. Many Nigerians lose money this way because the same password was used on a smaller site years ago. If your email leaked, change banking and payment passwords first, turn on two-factor authentication, and check statements for anything you do not recognise.

How do I keep my email safe from now on?

Use a long, unique password for your email and never reuse it. Turn on two-factor authentication. Be careful with links in unexpected emails, since phishing is still the easiest way to steal credentials. Keep your devices updated and only install software from official sources. Once in a while, scan your own website and domain too, since a compromised site can be used to phish your customers, and our free website security check guide explains what to look for.
